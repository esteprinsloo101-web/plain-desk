Burial Society Membership Form vs Contribution Sheet (South Africa)

Search for a burial society contribution sheet and you will mostly find membership forms and funeral-plan PDFs. Those documents matter. They are not the same job as a month-by-month contribution ledger.

This guide separates the two so a new secretary does not confuse “who joined” with “who paid”.

Plain Desk sells records templates only. Not funeral cover. Not a claims desk. Not a bank. Not a NASASA filing office.

Two documents, two jobs

DocumentJob it doesJob it does not do
Membership / application formCapture identity, beneficiaries, plan choice, joining rulesProve who paid every month
Contribution sheet / trackerLog paid / arrears / notes per cycleDecide claim amounts or replace an insurer

A well-run burial society usually needs both: a signed join record, plus a living sheet the treasurer updates after each collection meeting.

Why search results look “insurance-heavy”

Public web results for burial admin often surface society membership forms, funeral cover application PDFs, and generic fillable-form sites. Useful if you are joining a registered society or buying cover. Less useful if you already run a community burial stokvel and your fight is “Sipho says he paid in March”.

Those results are observations only — not rankings we invent or claim to beat. They show the intent mix: join forms vs books.

What belongs on a contribution sheet

  1. Member display name (IDs stay off the group chat — POPIA common sense)
  2. Cycle / month
  3. Amount due (ZAR)
  4. Amount paid + date
  5. Arrears / notes
  6. Good-standing flag for your own rules (not a legal insurance status)

Export a PDF for AGM or year-end minutes. The sheet does not pay claims.

Membership form still matters

Use (or keep) a membership form when someone joins mid-year, beneficiaries change, your constitution requires a signed application, or a bank / society scheme asks for a member list.

Do not treat the form as proof of every contribution. That is how WhatsApp arguments start.

Honest limits

ClaimTruth
“This sheet is funeral insurance”No
“This form pays the undertaker”No — your society process / insurer does
“Templates hold member money”No — your bank or cash process does
“We file NASASA for you”No

If your group also runs a grocery or savings share-out, keep those books separate from burial good-standing.

FAQ

Can we use Excel or Google Sheets?

Yes. Pick one master file the secretary owns.

Do we need an app?

Only if the committee will actually log in. Many groups keep a sheet for the audit trail.

Where does constitution wording fit?

Beside the books — not inside a membership PDF alone. A treasurer pack pairs constitution + tracker + fine/loan + year-end together.

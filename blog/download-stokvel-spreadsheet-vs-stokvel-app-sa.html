Honest limits (both sides)

A free tracker (Excel/Sheets) can track contributions and rotation well. Many groups stop there. Gaps show up at year-end, fines/loans, or when a new treasurer inherits three WhatsApp threads and no constitution.

An app can reduce “did you pay?” noise. It does not replace a written constitution, POPIA common sense, or dual-signatory banking discipline. It also does not make Plain Desk (or any template seller) a NASASA filing office or an insurer.

Burial societies: keep claim / good-standing books separate from grocery or savings share-outs. Templates are records, not funeral cover.

A simple decision rule

Choose a download pack if most of these are true:

  1. Your group already has (or will open) its own bank/society process.
  2. You want Excel or Google Sheets the secretary can export to PDF for minutes.
  3. You need constitution + member register + contribution tracker + fine/loan log + year-end payout in one purchase.
  4. You do not want another monthly SaaS bill before the first ZAR of admin stress is solved.

Explore an app if most of these are true:

  1. Members will actually log in every cycle (not only the treasurer).
  2. You want automated reminders more than offline files.
  3. Someone on the committee will own onboarding and permissions.
  4. You have already agreed how the app relates to your bank and constitution.

Many healthy clubs use both: sheets (or a pack) for the audit trail, WhatsApp for chat, and optionally an app for reminders — without mixing brands or promising rankings.

FAQ

Is a stokvel app better than a spreadsheet?

Neither is “better” in the abstract. Apps win on reminders and shared login. Spreadsheets win on ownership, offline meetings, and easy PDF minutes.

Can I start on a free tracker and upgrade later?

Yes. Start clean books now. If you outgrow a single grid, move to a fuller pack or an app — keep one master history.

Stokvel Two Cash Books / Dual Contribution Meeting (South Africa)

Two-pot fights sound simple until the room opens the notebooks: one member paid grocery but not savings, another paid one lump that someone wrote in both books, and a third wants year-end payout from “everything they ever paid.” The WhatsApp thread has three different totals and no shared line.

This guide is for SA savings clubs, grocery stokvels, and burial societies that run two cash books / dual contribution registers — for example grocery + savings, burial + savings, or any two pots — and need a shared dual-books meeting file before the room votes on transfers between pots, year-end math, or who is behind on which book.

Lead with the two cash books / dual contribution meeting job, not “spreadsheet vs free.” Free PDFs and free trackers stay useful. The gap is walking into the room with one shared structure so Book A and Book B stay separate and nobody double-counts a lump payment.

This is not the contribution arrears meeting. That agenda is who paid late or not at all on a single cycle pot. This agenda is two pots side by side — which payment belongs to which book, whether pots may cross-subsidise, and how year-end or exit math treats each register.

This is also not the joining-fee vs monthly dues meeting. That agenda is once-off join money vs recurring dues. Dual-books meetings argue grocery vs savings (or burial vs savings) columns that must not be mixed.

When you need this meeting

  1. Grocery + savings (or burial + savings) in one club — members pay two amounts, but one WhatsApp chat and one confused notebook.
  2. Lump payments — a member pays once; the room disagrees whether it covers pot A, pot B, or both.
  3. Year-end or mid-year payout — some want one combined total; the constitution (or practice) treats the pots as separate.
  4. Trust reset after a mix-up — money was moved between pots without a dated minute, or a treasurer kept only one total.

What belongs in the shared dual-books meeting file

  1. Constitution / club-rule placeholders your club fills — names of pot A and pot B, contribution amount per pot, whether pots may lend to each other, how year-end treats each pot, who signs for which account (if separate).
  2. Member register with join date and status (active / exiting / exited) — display names for the room; keep IDs off the group chat (POPIA common sense).
  3. Contribution calendar — Pot A (Excel / Google Sheets friendly) — cycle columns for pot A only (e.g. grocery or burial).
  4. Contribution calendar — Pot B — cycle columns for pot B only (e.g. savings). Same members, separate grid.
  5. Dual payment list for the cutoff date — member, amount paid, which pot(s), receipt/ref note, notes the secretary can read aloud (so lump payments are allocated once, not twice).
  6. Fine & loan log (if open) — tagged to pot A, pot B, or club admin so fines are not confused with either contribution book.
  7. Net / interim worksheet — pot A totals and pot B totals side by side before year-end, exit, or transfer votes.
  8. Short order of operations — freeze both books at cutoff → allocate each payment to one pot only (unless the minute says otherwise) → apply only rules already in the constitution → minute any pot-to-pot transfer → bank separately where the club uses separate accounts.

How this differs from a single “paid” column

A contribution tracker that marks “paid” this month on one grid does not answer whether grocery and savings (or burial and savings) stay separate, whether a lump was double-counted, or how each pot nets at year-end. A dual-books meeting also needs two calendars, the dual payment list, and the side-by-side net worksheet.

FAQ

Is this just another blank spreadsheet?

No. It is a meeting pack: pot-rule placeholders plus two contribution calendars, a dual payment list, and a side-by-side net worksheet so the room shares one cutoff-date structure.

We already use one tracker for everything — why buy a pack?

One grid that mixes grocery and savings still ends in “prove it from the chat.” The pack puts placeholders and separate books in one download. Free single-pot trackers stay free elsewhere. This is not a filing service and not a bank.

Is this the same as the arrears or joining-fee meeting guide?

No. Arrears meetings argue late or missing payments on a cycle. Joining-fee meetings argue once-off join money vs monthly dues. Dual-books meetings argue two pots side by side so the room does not mix or double-count. Many clubs run more than one agenda; use the guide that matches the fight.

Excel or Google Sheets?

Spreadsheet templates work in either. Pick one master file the secretary owns for the meeting date — with clear Pot A and Pot B sheets.

Do you hold our money?

No. Templates only. You keep banking and member data.

Stokvel Treasurer Duties in South Africa: Monthly Checklist

Often the treasurer gets the job at an AGM with one sentence: “You’re good with numbers.” Then the WhatsApp group starts sending proof-of-payment screenshots, someone asks for a balance, and nobody has written down what the treasurer is actually supposed to do each month.

This guide sets out stokvel treasurer duties in South Africa in plain terms: the monthly routine, the records worth keeping, what to do before payout time, and how to stay transparent with members. It works for savings clubs, grocery stokvels, rotating clubs and burial society bookkeeping.

First, the ground rule: your own stokvel constitution decides the treasurer’s duties. Nothing here overrides it. If your constitution is silent on something, raise it at a meeting and minute the decision. What does a stokvel treasurer do?

In most clubs the treasurer is the member who keeps the books. That usually means:

  • Recording every contribution — who paid, how much, on what date, and the reference.
  • Tracking what is still owed — missed contributions, fines and any member loans the constitution allows.
  • Keeping the member list current — joins, exits and status.
  • Reporting to members — a short statement at each meeting so everyone sees the same numbers.
  • Preparing payouts — working out who gets what under the club’s rules, for the committee and members to approve.
  • Handing over cleanly when a new treasurer takes over.

What the treasurer usually does not do alone: change contribution amounts, waive fines, approve loans or decide payout order. Those are group decisions under the constitution. Some constitutions also ask for two people to sign off on withdrawals — check yours.

Records a stokvel treasurer should keep

Your constitution may list specific records and how long to keep them. If it does not, these are the practical basics:

  1. Constitution — the signed, dated version, plus any minuted amendments.
  2. Member register — name, contact, join date, status (active / exiting / exited). Keep ID copies securely and off the group chat.
  3. Contribution calendar — one row per member per cycle: amount due, paid Y/N, date, reference.
  4. Fine & loan log — date, member, type, amount, reason, due date, settled Y/N.
  5. Bank statements or cash-box records — matched to the contribution calendar each cycle.
  6. Payout sheet — per member: total contributed, fines owed, loans outstanding, net payout, paid out Y/N.
  7. Meeting minutes — kept by the secretary, but the treasurer should have a copy of every money decision.

One master file, owned by the treasurer, with read-only copies or PDF exports for everyone else, avoids the “three different Excels” problem.

Treasurer duties at payout time

Whether your club pays out in rotation, at year-end, or in a December grocery share-out, the treasurer’s job is to put one agreed number per member in front of the room.

  1. Set a cut-off date for contributions counted in this payout (use the date in your constitution or minute one).
  2. Freeze the contribution calendar at that date and match it to the bank statement.
  3. Roll up fines and loans so each member has one outstanding figure.
  4. Work out net payout per member using only rules already in the constitution — for example, total contributed minus fines minus loans outstanding. Do not invent a formula on the day.
  5. Circulate the payout sheet before the meeting so members can query their own line.
  6. Get sign-off from the committee / members as your constitution requires, and minute it.
  7. Pay out through the club’s normal banking process, then mark each line paid with date and reference.

How a treasurer stays transparent with members

  • Same statement format every meeting, so changes are easy to spot.
  • Bank statement available at meetings for members who want to check (your constitution may give members an inspection right).
  • Private messages for private matters — arrears, loans and personal circumstances are not group-chat topics.
  • No decisions in WhatsApp alone — use the chat for reminders; take money decisions to a meeting and minute them.
  • Dual control where you can — a second signatory or a committee member who checks the payout sheet.
  • Clean handover — when you step down, hand over the master file, statements, minutes and a list of open items at a meeting.

FAQ

What are the main duties of a stokvel treasurer?

Recording every contribution, tracking arrears, fines and loans, keeping the member register current, reporting a statement at meetings, and preparing payouts for the group to approve. Your stokvel constitution is the final word on the treasurer’s duties.

Does the treasurer decide fines, loans or payout order?

Usually not alone. Those are group decisions under your constitution. The treasurer records them and applies the rules the group has agreed and minuted.

Is a WhatsApp screenshot enough proof of payment?

Screenshots help, but match every payment to the bank statement or cash-box record before you mark it paid. The statement is the record the room can check.
